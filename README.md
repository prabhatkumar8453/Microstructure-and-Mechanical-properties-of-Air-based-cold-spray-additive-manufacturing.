# Microstructure & Mechanical Properties of Air-Based Cold Spray Additive Manufacturing

## 📌 Overview

This project investigates the **microstructure and mechanical properties of air-based cold spray additive manufacturing (CSAM) deposits**. Cold spray is a solid-state additive manufacturing technique in which powder particles are accelerated to high velocity and deposited onto a substrate through severe plastic deformation without melting.

The study focuses on understanding the effect of air-based cold spraying and subsequent heat treatment on the microstructure, phase composition, inter-splat bonding, friction behaviour, and hardness of the deposited material.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

The main objectives of this study are:

- To understand the fundamentals of **thermal spray and cold spray processes**.
- To investigate the feasibility of **air-based cold spray additive manufacturing**.
- To characterize the microstructure of cold-sprayed deposits.
- To study the effect of **heat treatment at 400°C for 1 hour**.
- To analyze phase composition using **X-Ray Diffraction (XRD)**.
- To investigate microstructure and elemental composition using **FE-SEM coupled with EDS**.
- To evaluate inter-splat bonding using **scratch testing**.
- To determine the mechanical properties using **microhardness testing**.

---

## 🔬 Cold Spray Process

Cold spray is a **solid-state additive manufacturing process** in which powder particles are accelerated to supersonic velocity and bonded to the substrate through plastic deformation without melting.

The basic process consists of:

1. Powder feeding
2. High-pressure gas acceleration
3. Particle acceleration through a spray nozzle
4. High-velocity impact with the substrate
5. Plastic deformation and bonding
6. Formation of the deposited layer

Unlike conventional thermal spray processes, cold spray avoids melting of the feedstock material.

---

## 🌬️ Why Air-Based Cold Spray?

Air-based cold spray was investigated because of its:

- Simpler equipment setup
- Lower operating cost
- Economic feasibility
- Ability to provide sufficient particle velocity for materials such as **Al, Cu, and Zn**

### Limitations

The main limitations associated with air-based cold spray include:

- Possibility of oxidation
- Lower particle velocity compared with high-pressure systems
- Potential reduction in coating strength

## 🔎 XRD Analysis

X-Ray Diffraction was used to investigate the phase composition of the aluminium powder, as-sprayed deposit, and heat-treated deposit.

The aluminium powder exhibited an **FCC crystal structure** with the corresponding planes:

- (111)
- (200)
- (220)
- (311)
- (222)

The as-sprayed sample showed slightly broadened and reduced-intensity peaks, which were associated with deformation during the cold spray process.

After heat treatment, the peaks became sharper and more intense, indicating recovery of the crystalline structure.

No unwanted intermetallic phase formation was observed at the selected heat-treatment condition.

---

## 🔬 FE-SEM & EDS Characterization

FE-SEM was used to investigate the microstructure of the as-sprayed and heat-treated deposits.

EDS analysis was used for elemental characterization of the deposited material.

The characterization provides information about:

- Particle deformation
- Deposit morphology
- Inter-splat regions
- Elemental distribution
- Microstructural changes after heat treatment

---

## 🧷 Scratch Testing

Scratch testing was performed to investigate the bonding behaviour of the deposited layers.

### Test Parameters

- Scratch length: **3 mm**
- Scratch velocity: **0.2 mm/s**
- Applied load: **20 N**

The scratch tracks of the as-sprayed and heat-treated samples were compared to investigate changes in inter-splat bonding.

The coefficient of friction (COF) was also obtained from the scratch test.

---

## ⚙️ Microhardness Testing

Microhardness measurements were performed using a **Vickers hardness testing machine**.

### Test Parameters

- Load: **0.3 kg**
- Dwell time: **10 seconds**

### Hardness Results

| Condition | Average Hardness |
|-----------|------------------|
| As-Sprayed | **85.04 HV** |
| Heat Treated (400°C / 1 hr) | **42.84 HV** |

The measured hardness values were used to evaluate the effect of heat treatment on the mechanical properties of the deposited material.

---

## 🔥 Heat Treatment

The deposited samples were heat treated at:

**400°C for 1 hour**

The heat-treatment study was performed to investigate its effect on:

- Inter-splat bonding
- Microstructure
- Crystal structure
- Friction behaviour
- Hardness

The study observed changes in the deposited material after heat treatment, including recovery of the crystalline structure.

---

## 📊 Key Findings

The major observations from the study are:

- Air-based cold spray can produce dense deposits with relatively low thermal damage.
- Cold spray is a solid-state process that helps maintain the original phase of the feedstock.
- Heat treatment at **400°C for 1 hour** influences inter-splat bonding.
- XRD analysis indicates that the original crystal structure of the aluminium powder is retained.
- Heat treatment results in sharper and more intense XRD peaks.
- FE-SEM provides information about the microstructure of the deposited layers.
- Scratch testing was used to evaluate inter-splat bonding.
- The average measured hardness was **85.04 HV for the as-sprayed sample** and **42.84 HV after heat treatment**.
